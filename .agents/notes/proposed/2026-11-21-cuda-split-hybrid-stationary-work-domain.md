# Decision: Qualify a stationary work domain before split-hybrid Hessians

Status: proposed
Date: 2026-11-21

## Problem

The CUDA M06-2X and MN15 point programs follow Libxc's MGGA worker. They clip
the work coordinates, integrate the resulting energy per particle against the
physical density, and return potentials differentiated at the work coordinates,
not through the clipping map. An interior `fxc` action is not the derivative of
this potential in the physical coordinates. Even differentiating the actual
potential through clipping does not in general give a symmetric energy Hessian.

## Evidence

With the pinned PySCF Libxc 7.0.0 oracle, evaluate polarized correlation at
`(rho_a, rho_b, sigma_aa, sigma_ab, sigma_bb, tau_a, tau_b)` =
`(0.2, 2.5e-13, 0.01, 0, 0, 0.3, 8e-20)` for M06-2X. These Gram invariants
are physical and both spin channels satisfy the Fermi-hole bound. In
`tools.validate_split_hybrid_cuda.reference_points`, use code
`,MGGA_C_M06_2X` and central density steps `1e-5` and `rho_b/8`. Both minority
displacements remain below the correlation cutoff `1e-12`, so the majority
potential is unchanged: `d_b v_a = 0`. Both majority displacements change the
minority potential: `d_a v_b = -2.22742249`. The correlation-only MN15 probe
with `rho_b = 2.5e-16` and cutoff `1e-15` gives `0` and `-3150.2942`,
respectively. The probe does not use GPU execution or numerical SCF.

### M06-2X point prototype

For the first development candidate, use spinwise C2 exchange activation, a
C2 kinetic floor, and a correlation density floor at twice its source cutoff
so the continued inputs do not cross the internal density screen. Rescale the
correlation energy back to the physical total density before differentiating.
Keep both diagonal sigmas as squared Cartesian gradient norms. In the pinned
correlation graph, the remaining `sqrt(sigma)` nodes occur only in the square
of the scaled gradient in `D_sigma`; replace those two products by
`sigma * density_scale^2` before AD. Without this cancellation, AD at exactly
zero gradient evaluates a negative power of zero. Flooring sigma instead
incorrectly flattens the gradient-coordinate curvature there (observed action
difference `0.1031` from the nearby interior in the test direction).

The C++ FP64 probe generated by `ScalarCEmitter` matched Libxc energy,
potential and analytic action at 34 interior/equal-spin points within
`3.71e-14` maximum absolute difference. It also passed directional finite
differences and raw symmetry checks for one empty-spin, one flat-gradient,
and one tail point. The development script and compile receipts are under
`build/hessian-gpu-sm120/r2scan-orbital-f/`. An RTX 5090 Slurm run replayed
73 point/direction combinations and independently passed the same Libxc,
finite-difference and symmetry gates. The tighter host/device parity probe
(`rtol=2e-10`) did not pass for 12 repeats of the tail majority `v_tau`;
its largest relative discrepancy was `3.55e-10`. Keep that discrepancy
separate from the scientific gates. This is not endpoint qualification:
the extreme spin-ratio guard, the complete transition neighborhoods, and the
SCF/force/Hessian reuse of this energy still need separate validation.

### Isolated compiler and grid implementation

The development patch `m062x-stationary-point-and-grid.patch` under the same
build directory passes `git apply --check` against the worktree. Keep it
separate from the in-flight manylinux qualification to avoid mixing compiler
inputs. It shares C2 continuation primitives with r²SCAN; the r²SCAN response
header is byte-identical before and after extraction. The M06-2X correlation
floor also includes `2^-46` times the total density to keep extreme spin ratios
away from the pinned spin guard; compile-time point probes cover a majority
density of `2e6` with an empty minority channel.

Five compiled host point tests passed, including analytic Libxc values and
actions for 35 interior points, plus finite differences, spin exchange and
symmetry at the boundaries. Slurm job `1717` (RTX 5090) passed eight native
grid cases, crossing RKS/UKS with M06-2X and r²SCAN. The probe checked the
stationary energy, AO potential, fixed-density action, moving-grid action,
bilateral mixed contraction and isolated quadrature-weight term against
independent Libxc-based values. M06-2X did not reserve or traverse a nonlocal
pair domain. The compiled grid entry does not admit a molecular final state:
SCF domain selection, force binding and the final-state validator must still
be wired and qualified together.

The cumulative `m062x-stationary-through-point-binding.patch` also compiles
the opt-in first-order point launcher and rejects the incumbent split-hybrid
domain at Hessian response admission. `GENERATIVEQC_ENABLE_M062X_STATIONARY_POINT`
is OFF by default and does not select the point in the molecular SCF owner.
The configured NVIDIA build graph includes both point generation rules. In
Slurm job `1718`, the compiled first-order dispatch matched the independent
Libxc weighted energy and weak-form coefficients to `1.11e-16` (RKS) and
`1.04e-17` (UKS). No legacy response callback is bound to this private key.
The next integration must select this identity before SCF, bind the generated
force profile to it, and authenticate it in the final-state validator before
enabling the public Hessian path. The standalone dispatch proof does not
qualify that molecular path.

### Staged molecular binding and MN15 point check

The cumulative `m062x-stationary-through-molecular-binding.patch` leaves the
public functional code unchanged and binds strict-FP64 fused SCF, analytic
force and Hessian response to the same private M06-2X identity. Selection
requires canonical matched full-range J/K (Direct or DF), an all-electron
basis within the force inventory, Becke-3 partitioning and no application
precision budget. It depends on native force and Hessian AOT and does not
vary with the requested observable or transient storage. The final-state
validator and Hessian matcher authenticate the composition and refuse the
legacy domain. Both spin-specific force wrappers emit the same physical
point program as the SCF owner, with the kinetic coefficient converted to
the force weak form. The Python and CLI Hessian frontends require the linked
opt-in domain and authenticate it on the accepted SCF generation. The initial
experimental public scope was s-only orbital and explicit s-only DF auxiliary
shells. The presence of higher-angular kernels alone was not molecular
qualification.

Targeted host/CUDA objects compiled under `sccache 0.16.0`, including both
variational force bindings, the SCF dispatch and final-state validator, and
the Hessian response matcher. Compiler ownership checks passed. The staged
host run yielded 40 passes and one failure also reproduced on the unmodified
base: the older source-extraction fixture lacks the r²SCAN declarations.
A compiled admission probe accepted both spins with matched Direct/DF J/K
and rejected an incorrect exchange fraction, mixed J/K approximations,
non-Becke-3 partitioning, ECP contexts, angular overflow, AUTO precision and
an application precision budget.
The independent PySCF/Libxc probe reconverged all reference cases
(RKS/UKS × Direct/DF). RTX 5090 Slurm jobs 1727 and 1728 passed the force,
raw matrix symmetry, translation, HVP replay, reconverged directional
difference and independent complete-Hessian gates. Maximum element errors
were `9.51e-8` for H₂/H₂⁺ and `4.48e-7 Ha/Bohr²` for HeH⁺/HeH. The fitted
cases used the s subset of the JKFIT basis; the development AOT inventory
correctly rejected the full auxiliary set. These jobs do not qualify
higher-angular shells or a default-on release. Near-symmetry RHS columns
around `1e-17`–`9e-16` exposed the need for the `1e-12` default absolute
response tolerance already used in native GMRES. Explicit zero remains
available to callers requesting a purely relative stop.
Slurm job `1730` repeated the eight independent comparisons through the
public Python Hessian/HVP and passed the four Python/CLI parity cases. The
non-wheel CLI must load the matched CUDA provider; job `1729` reached Python
results but used the node's older cuSOLVER for the CLI and failed to resolve
the batched eigensolver symbol. Job `1730` used the installed CUDA 12.9
provider and preserved Slurm's device assignment.
The separate `m062x-build-option-off-gating.patch` excludes the response
generation and instantiation when the option is off and is applied. Both CUDA
object variants compiled; symbol inspection found both M06-2X point
instantiations with the option on and neither with the option off, with the
r²SCAN instantiations retained.

MN15 has no reachable `sqrt(sigma_aa)` or `sqrt(sigma_bb)` in its pinned
polarized energy after existing importer reductions. The M06-2X-specific
`D_sigma` rewrite must not be applied to it. An isolated compiled MN15 point
probe reused the same continuation without that rewrite; 34 interior,
equal-spin and flat-gradient cases matched Libxc energy/potential/action to
`6.57e-14` maximum absolute error. One tail, empty-spin and extreme-spin
probe each passed directional finite differences and scaled Hessian
symmetry. For the tail symmetry check, an absolute asymmetry of `0.109375`
occurred on very large density curvature entries; the test compared relative
error there. Slurm job `1719` replayed all 73 point evaluations on RTX 5090;
interior values matched Libxc within `6.48e-14`, and the same grad/action
and scaled symmetry tests passed at the boundaries. This is point evidence
only, not MN15 response admission. The shared compiler emitter now admits
MN15 with an explicit flat-gradient graph guard and maps its point potential
into the force weak form. The M06-2X emitted CUDA point header and moving-grid
source were byte-identical after the factoring. Compiled point-action and
force-transport checks against Libxc 7.0 cover both methods.

MN15 now has its own opt-in CUDA molecular binding and distinct final-state
domain. It uses the same admission predicate as M06-2X but the compiler
registry supplies the method's own exact-exchange fraction. An old split
domain or the other method's variational domain cannot authorize response.
With both options on, Slurm job `1731` passed all eight Python/CLI parity
cases and repeated the independent PySCF/Libxc tests in each method's RKS/UKS
and Direct/s-only DF modes. The maximum MN15 Hessian errors were `3.11e-7`
for H₂/H₂⁺ and `3.62e-7 Ha/Bohr²` for HeH⁺/HeH. Raw programs, test
commands and receipts are in the ignored `r2scan-orbital-f` build records.

The spherical cc-pVDZ and full JKFIT qualification is recorded in
[`2026-11-21-cuda-density-fitting-cotangent-order.md`](../implemented/numerics/2026-11-21-cuda-density-fitting-cotangent-order.md).
The public gate now admits spherical d orbital and g auxiliary shells within
the linked derivative inventory and resource bounds; it still excludes ECP,
Cartesian d and higher, and incumbent nonvariational point domains.

## Proposed direction

- Derive the energy, potential and directional potential from one physical
  scalar graph, sharing the pinned interior and the stabilized M06-2X Stoll
  term. Audit internal spin screens as well as the outer work wrapper. A smooth
  outer floor does not repair an interior discontinuity crossed by that floor.
- Qualify both restricted and unrestricted point evaluation, including
  empty-spin, equal-spin, flat-gradient, and below/above-cutoff neighborhoods.
  Gate against independent Libxc values and analytic actions in the interior;
  where the policy differs, gate against derivatives of the continued energy
  and check both mixed symmetry and two-sided continuity.
- Bind the qualified point domain before SCF and carry its identity through
  forces and response. Require independent reconverged molecular energies,
  forces, full Hessians and HVPs before exposing a public method. Keep the
  incumbent domain for contexts not admitted by the new point model.

## Rejected alternative

Do not admit a public Hessian by selecting the incumbent split-hybrid SCF point
and adding a Libxc-style second derivative. The counterexample is a mismatch in
the differentiated equations, not a response-solver tolerance problem. See the
[r²SCAN deployment](../implemented/numerics/2026-11-20-cuda-r2scan-variational-deployment.md)
for the precedent, not evidence that these two methods are already qualified.
